Apps I use

These are the phone apps that have actually earned the space on my home screen. I test new ones every trip, and most get deleted. The ones below stayed because they made something easier or safer on the road.

Weather and translate are already on the phone by default — everyone uses those. The list below is the extras I install on top.

AllTrails

Hiking trail discovery. Reviews, photos, and current conditions on trails I haven't been on yet. Most of my trail picking starts here.

Why I keep it: I find a trail on AllTrails, download the GPX file, and upload it into Guru Maps Pro. AllTrails for research, Guru Maps for actual navigation once I'm off cell.

Guru Maps Pro

Offline topo maps. I keep the maps of everywhere I'm going downloaded before I leave cell range. Free version is fine for casual use, Pro is worth it if you do real backcountry or off-road driving.

Why I keep it: Offline maps + GPX from AllTrails. I save all the spots I want to go to as saved locations — great for road trips when I don't have reception.

Watch Duty

Wildfire alerts. Pulls from official fire sources and pushes notifications when there's a fire within a radius you set. Free, no ads, runs on donations.

Why I keep it: Before I camp somewhere I follow that area in the app so I get notified if a fire breaks out nearby. I check the map beforehand to see what's going on, then I trust the notifications.

Slopes

Ski and snowboard tracker. Records runs with speed, distance, vertical, lift time vs. slope time. Apple Watch support so you don't need to keep your phone out.

Why I keep it: The social layer — you can follow your friends and see where they are on the mountain in real time. Makes a ski day way more fun when the group is split across the mountain.

Our Groceries

Shared grocery list. Real-time sync across phones and the web, so everyone in the household sees the same list at the same time.

Why I keep it: If I go shopping with multiple people, everyone has the app open and sees what's already in the cart — it updates live. No more "did we already get eggs?"

Packing Pro

Trip packing lists. Per-trip lists, per-person lists, per-item details like weight, value, and priority.

Why I keep it: I have different packing lists for different purposes and check those off before each trip to make sure I have everything I need. The kind of thing that means I never forget the camp-stove fuel on a 4-day trip.

Files

The stock iOS Files app. Reads, stores, and syncs PDFs and other documents locally and through iCloud.

Why I keep it: When I buy tickets for events or transportation, I save the PDFs/screenshots locally in Files. Not every venue or operator supports Apple Wallet, and I want them somewhere reliable. Sensitive stuff — IDs, insurance, passports — I keep in a separate secure location, not in Files.

Photos

Stock iOS Photos app. Stores, edits, and organizes photos and videos.

Why I keep it: I use all of it — albums, shared albums with family, and editing on the phone. It's the only place I trust with the originals.

Audible

Audiobooks. Download titles for offline listening.

Why I keep it: Long train rides and plane rides — I download a book before I go. Whatever I'm in the mood for when the trip starts.

Music

Stock Apple Music. Downloaded playlists and albums for offline playback.

Why I keep it: Same purpose as Audible — download music before the plane and have something to listen to when there's no signal.

Kindle

E-reader. Downloaded books from Amazon and from my local library.

Why I keep it: Reading on the road. I often borrow library ebooks about the trip destination — the history, the guidebook, the novel set there — and download them so I have reading material offline.

GPS My City

Self-guided city walking tours. Designed for the "I want more background info but I don't want to book a guided tour" case.

Why I keep it: When I want a deeper sense of a city than just walking around. Pulls up a route with the history and context of what I'm passing.

GuideAlong

Audio tour guides for driving. Same idea as GPS My City but for road trips — local history narrated as you drive past it.

Why I keep it: The driving version. National parks and long western drives especially — works offline once downloaded.

Local transit apps

City-specific public transportation apps. I install them depending on where I'm going.

Why I keep it: Transit is different in every city — Tokyo, London, and the rest all need their own apps. I add what I need per trip and delete what I don't.

Last updated: July 2026. I'll add to this list as I try new apps that earn the space.
